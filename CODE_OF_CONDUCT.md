# SimProof Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to making participation in SimProof respectful and free from
harassment for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience, education, socioeconomic
status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, sexual or
romantic identity, or orientation.

We will act in ways that support an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy
community.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and constructive.
- Assume good intent while addressing concrete impact.
- Accept correction and take responsibility for mistakes.
- Keep technical disagreement focused on evidence and project contracts.
- Respect privacy and do not publish personal, student, security, or confidential data.
- Credit contributors and scientific or educational sources accurately.
- Put the safety and well-being of the community ahead of individual preferences.

## Unacceptable behavior

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention;
- discriminatory, insulting, demeaning, or inflammatory comments;
- deliberate misgendering or use of rejected names;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- sustained disruption of discussion, review, or project infrastructure;
- retaliation against someone who raises a concern; or
- other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a
  professional community.

Vulnerability details, credentials, student-identifying information, prompts,
generated content, provider responses, and raw diagnostics must never be used to prove
a point in a public forum.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
project-operated communication spaces, and public spaces when someone represents the
project or its community.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not use a public issue or GitHub vulnerability advisory for a conduct report.
Security advisories are reserved for vulnerabilities and are not a private conduct
channel.

No private conduct-reporting channel is configured in this repository at present.
Before publication, the operator must update this section with a dedicated private
channel and identify the maintainers responsible for receiving conduct reports. The
channel may be an access-controlled mailbox or private form, but it must be distinct
from vulnerability reporting and must not expose reports to the public.

Until that channel is configured, do not send incident details through repository
features. Use an already established private channel only to ask the operator to
provide the dedicated conduct-reporting path, without describing the incident. This is
a pre-publication prerequisite, not a claim that a reporting channel currently exists.

Maintainers responsible for enforcement must protect the reporter's privacy, avoid
conflicts of interest, assess the context and impact, and apply a proportionate
response. Responses may include a private correction, formal warning, removal of
content, restriction of project access, or permanent removal from project spaces.
Maintainers who are involved in a report must recuse themselves from deciding it.

Bad-faith or knowingly false reports may themselves violate this policy. A report that
cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, a bad-faith report.
